Sound familiar?

It's probably not how you slept "wrong" one night

For most people, morning neck stiffness comes from the same few habits repeated every night.

You wake up stiff

Turning your head hurts for the first hour of the day, then slowly eases off.

You flip all night

You keep folding and punching your pillow, trying to find a comfortable angle.

Your pillow goes flat

It feels fine at bedtime, but by 3 a.m. your head has sunk and your neck is bent.

Why it happens

Your neck needs to stay in a straight line all night

Standing up, your head sits balanced on your spine. Lying down, it should stay in that same neutral line. A pillow that's too high pushes your head up; one that's too flat lets it drop. Either way, the muscles on one side of your neck stay stretched for hours.

Side sleepers

Need the gap between ear and shoulder filled, so the head doesn't tilt down.

Back sleepers

Need support under the curve of the neck, without pushing the chin forward.

Combination sleepers

Need a pillow that works in every position, so they stop waking up to adjust.

Stomach sleepers

Twist the neck for hours. Many find it easier to switch to side sleeping.

How to choose

3 things a neck-friendly pillow should have

  • A contoured shape. Higher under the neck, lower under the head, to keep everything aligned.
  • Foam that holds its height. Dense memory foam doesn't flatten during the night like fiber fill.
  • Zones for different positions. So it still supports you when you roll from your back to your side.

3 habits that help, whatever pillow you use

  • Replace pillows that no longer spring back. Most lose their support after a year or two.
  • Avoid long stretches looking down at your phone right before bed.
  • Keep your shoulders below the pillow. It should support your head and neck, not your shoulders.
